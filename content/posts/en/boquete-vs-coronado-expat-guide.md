<p>When an expat decides to settle in Panama outside the capital, two names come up on almost every shortlist: Boquete, in the highlands of Chiriquí, and Coronado, on the Pacific coast. Both have established international communities, services built with foreigners in mind, and a gentler pace than the city — but they answer two very different ideas of how to live.</p>
<p>Climate draws the first big line between them. Boquete sits at around 1,200 metres and enjoys spring weather all year, with cool evenings that call for a fireplace and mornings wrapped in the region's characteristic <em>bajareque</em> mist. Coronado is beach tropics through and through: heat, sun and the sea a few steps away, ideal for anyone who pictures coastal living and water sports twelve months a year.</p>
<p>On community and cost of living, Boquete draws mostly retirees and digital nomads who value nature, high-altitude coffee and a somewhat gentler cost of living. Coronado, closer to the capital at barely an hour and a half down the highway, offers better access to hospitals, shopping centres and international flights, in exchange for somewhat higher property and service prices. For many buyers, that proximity to the city is its decisive advantage.</p>
<p>There is no single right answer: the choice comes down to mountain or beach, quiet or closeness to the city. What both destinations share is mature infrastructure for foreign residents and a quality of life that is hard to match in the region.
